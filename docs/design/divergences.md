# Recorded divergences from DSS

This register lists every case in which sigillum intentionally produces a different result from DSS. The process is described in [conformance.md](conformance.md).

| Id | Format | Description | Standard clause | Test vector | Upstream report | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| DIV-1 | JAdES | DSS accepts detached signatures with the `ObjectIdByURI` mechanism over more than one object. sigillum reports INDETERMINATE with "not supported", because the boundary between objects is not signed. Class 3, intentional restriction ([ADR-0019](adr/0019-divergence-classes-and-intentional-restrictions.md)), decided in [ADR-0018](adr/0018-restrict-jades-payloads-and-detached-mechanisms.md) | ETSI TS 119 182-1 V1.2.1, clause 5.2.8.3.2 | To be added with the J5 fixture | Report to ETSI TC ESI drafted, not yet sent; optional under ADR-0019 | Accepted; fixture pending (J5) |
| DIV-2 | All formats | DSS is expected to accept object identifiers with an arc of 2^28 or more, such as `2.25` followed by a UUID. DSS 6.5.1 reads them through Bouncy Castle 1.86, whose `ASN1ObjectIdentifier` holds large arcs as `BigInteger` values. It accepts arcs of any size within its limit of 4096 contents octets. The DIV-2 fixture is to confirm it. sigillum reports INDETERMINATE with "not supported", in dotted text and in DER. `const-oid` 0.9.6 mishandles such arcs, as [RustCrypto/formats#1585](https://github.com/RustCrypto/formats/issues/1585) shows; [RustCrypto/formats#1592](https://github.com/RustCrypto/formats/pull/1592), [RustCrypto/formats#1600](https://github.com/RustCrypto/formats/pull/1600) and [RustCrypto/formats#1753](https://github.com/RustCrypto/formats/pull/1753) fixed the dotted form on master. In dotted form, an arc of 2^28 or more that fits in 32 bits can parse and then panic on display or arc iteration. The arc 2^28 is encoded as 0, so `1.2.268435456` gives `1.2.0`. Above 32 bits, release builds wrap silently, so `1.2.4294967301` gives `1.2.5`. In DER, an arc above 32 bits can be truncated silently, so `2A 90 80 80 80 05` gives `1.2.5` ([RustCrypto/formats#2412](https://github.com/RustCrypto/formats/pull/2412)). Class 3, intentional restriction ([ADR-0019](adr/0019-divergence-classes-and-intentional-restrictions.md)), decided in [ADR-0024](adr/0024-bounded-decoding-of-untrusted-input.md). The limit also guards against the truncation in DER. It is lifted only when a `const-oid` release used by sigillum contains all five fixes: [RustCrypto/formats#1522](https://github.com/RustCrypto/formats/pull/1522), [RustCrypto/formats#1592](https://github.com/RustCrypto/formats/pull/1592), [RustCrypto/formats#1600](https://github.com/RustCrypto/formats/pull/1600), [RustCrypto/formats#1753](https://github.com/RustCrypto/formats/pull/1753) and [RustCrypto/formats#2412](https://github.com/RustCrypto/formats/pull/2412). This applies to the DER path and to the dotted path alike. Lifting it relaxes the four-octet rule for subidentifiers of the walk and the arc limit of the dotted syntax. The syntax and round-trip checks of ADR-0024 stay. Under the general rule of ADR-0024, rule 4, any untrusted identifier that fails those checks or parsing by `const-oid` also gives "not supported". This includes the structural limits of `const-oid` 0.9.6: 3 to 39 contents octets, and a second arc of at most 39 under root 2 as well | ITU-T X.660 (07/2011), clause 7.6, leaves arcs unbounded; ITU-T X.690 (02/2021), clause 8.19 | To be added with the first story that parses untrusted object identifiers, in DER or dotted text, in practice the first that decodes untrusted DER | None needed for DSS; the defects are public upstream | Accepted; fixture pending |
| DIV-3 | X.509, OCSP | DSS 6.5.1 accepts an OCSP response signed by a certificate that is neither the CA nor a delegated responder with `id-kp-OCSPSigning`, once it has revocation data for that signer, and reports PASSED. sigillum rejects the response. Class 1, required by the standard ([ADR-0019](adr/0019-divergence-classes-and-intentional-restrictions.md)): DSS accepts what the standard forbids. No constraint of the DSS 6.5.1 policy schema can require `id-kp-OCSPSigning` of delegated responders only, so the aligned policy cannot resolve it ([ADR-0023](adr/0023-validation-policy-of-the-dss-oracle.md)) | RFC 6960, section 3.2, item 4, and section 4.2.2.2 | `ocsp/responder-without-ocsp-signing` given with `crl/ca-ecdsa`, which covers its signer `pki/signer-p384` (`ValidateCertificateTest`) | Required; drafted, not yet sent | Recorded; report pending |
| DIV-4 | X.509, OCSP, CRL | When a supplied OCSP response covers the certificate but gives the status unknown, or its signer cannot be validated because the certificate of the responder carried in the response has an altered signature, DSS 6.5.1 does not use a supplied CRL that covers the same certificate. It reports INDETERMINATE with CERTIFICATE_CHAIN_GENERAL_FAILURE, while the CRL shows the certificate not revoked. When the response's own signature does not verify, DSS sets the response aside and uses the CRL, so that case is not part of this divergence. sigillum uses another acceptable source of revocation data when one exists, and reports PASSED in these cases. Class 2, DSS behaviour appears incorrect | ETSI EN 319 102-1 V1.4.1, clause 5.2.6.4, step 4 | `ocsp/unknown`, or `ocsp/signer-p256` with the signature of the responder certificate in its `certs` altered, each given with `crl/ca-ecdsa` (`ValidateCertificateTest`) | Required; drafted, not yet sent | Recorded; report pending |
| DIV-5 | X.509 | For an expired certificate whose revocation data were issued at or before its `notAfter` and show it not revoked, DSS 6.5.1 reports INDETERMINATE with OUT_OF_BOUNDS_NOT_REVOKED. Such data show no status after the certificate expired: no CRL carries expiredCertsOnCRL and no response archiveCutoff. NOTE 8 of clause 5.2.6.4, which is informative, describes a certificate known not to have been revoked as one whose CA keeps providing status after expiry, checked, or whose CA assures that it was not revoked at signing time, so sigillum predicts OUT_OF_BOUNDS_NO_POE. A CRL with expiredCertsOnCRL (ITU-T X.509 (10/2019), clause 9.5.2.8) gives OUT_OF_BOUNDS_NOT_REVOKED in both. Class 2, DSS behaviour appears inconsistent with NOTE 8 | ETSI EN 319 102-1 V1.4.1, clause 5.2.6.4, step 7 and NOTE 8 | `pki/signer-expired`, `ocsp/signer-expired`, `pki/ca-expired`, `ocsp/signer-expired-before-expiry`; `crl/ca-ecdsa-expired-certs` as the control (`ValidateCertificateTest`, `sigillum-testpki verify --dss` and the differential tests of `sigillum-oracle`, for the four, which name DIV-5 in `dss-divergence`) | Required; drafted, not yet sent | Recorded; report pending |
| DIV-6 | JAdES | When the JWS Protected Header of an attached JAdES signature in the JWS compact serialisation has changed after signing and its payload has not, DSS 6.5.1 reports TOTAL_FAILED with HASH_FAILURE. It treats the JWS Signing Input as a reference whose data is not intact. Table 15 of clause 5.2.7.3, like Table 6, gives HASH_FAILURE when the hash of a signed data item does not match the corresponding hash value in the signature. Such a JWS carries no hash value of its payload: its signature value covers the JWS Signing Input, protected header included (RFC 7515, section 5.2, step 8). ETSI TS 119 182-1 V1.2.1, clause 3.1, defines the JWS Signature Value as calculated over octets "derived from the JWS Protected Header and data to be signed", so the header is not a signed data object, and the payload, which is, did not change. Clause 5.2.7.4 leaves how the signed data are obtained and checked, its first two steps, to the signature type, and refers to the CAdES, XAdES and PAdES standards and RFC 3852, not to ETSI TS 119 182-1. sigillum reports the failed verification of the signature value, step 4: SIG_CRYPTO_FAILURE. Class 2, DSS behaviour appears incorrect ([ADR-0019](adr/0019-divergence-classes-and-intentional-restrictions.md)) | ETSI EN 319 102-1 V1.4.1, clause 5.2.7.3, Table 15, clause 5.2.7.4, steps 2 and 4, and clause 5.1.3, Table 6; ETSI TS 119 182-1 V1.2.1, clause 3.1 | `mutations/jades-tampered-header` (`sigillum-testpki verify --dss` and the differential tests of `sigillum-oracle`) | Required; drafted, not yet sent | Recorded; report pending |
| DIV-7 | PAdES | When a PAdES document is changed in an incremental update after the signed revision, DSS 6.5.1 reports TOTAL_PASSED under the aligned policy, with warnings only. The page content changed (`mutations/pades-incremental-update`), the overlay over a line of text emptied (`mutations/pades-shadow-hide`) and the prepared page tree made the page tree of the catalog (`mutations/pades-shadow-hide-and-replace`) give BBB_FC_IVDBSFR_ANS (a visual difference) and BBB_FC_DSCNUOM_ANS (undefined object modifications after the signature revision); the value and appearance of a text field replaced (`mutations/pades-shadow-replace`) give BBB_FC_IVDBSFR_ANS. The signature and its `ByteRange` cover the signed revision, which is intact, and ISO 32000-1:2008, clause 7.5.6, lets a signed file be updated. This entry is provisional: the result that sigillum gives for such changes is decided by the phase 2 ADR on revision analysis ([ADR-0015](adr/0015-pdf-reader-and-incremental-writer.md)), with threats D1, D2 and D13 of the [threat model](threat-model.md). That ADR has two options: (a) TOTAL-FAILED; (b) TOTAL-PASSED for the signed revision, with a mandatory machine-readable conformance finding for the changes after it ([ADR-0017](adr/0017-defensive-api-rules.md), rule 4: findings never change the indication; threat R2: results identify the signed revision and its byte range). Under option (b), no divergence from DSS remains, and DIV-7 is withdrawn. The aligned policy of [ADR-0023](adr/0023-validation-policy-of-the-dss-oracle.md) is not changed for it, so the manifest records the result of DSS as `expected-dss`. Class to be decided by the phase 2 ADR on revision analysis ([ADR-0019](adr/0019-divergence-classes-and-intentional-restrictions.md)) | ISO 32000-1:2008, clause 7.5.6; ETSI EN 319 142-1 V1.2.1, clause 6.3, item k) | `mutations/pades-incremental-update`, `mutations/pades-shadow-hide`, `mutations/pades-shadow-replace`, `mutations/pades-shadow-hide-and-replace` (`sigillum-testpki verify --dss` and the differential tests of `sigillum-oracle`, which give TOTAL_PASSED) | None; to be decided with the class | Provisional; class and result of sigillum decided in phase 2 |

An artefact of the corpus on which DSS gives the result of a divergence names its entry, and that result, in the field `dss-divergence` of `testdata/manifest.toml`, described in `testdata/README.md`. The field is the only list of these artefacts. `sigillum-testpki verify --dss`, the differential tests of `sigillum-oracle` and `ValidateCertificateTest` of `tools/dss-oracle` read it, and a test of `sigillum-testpki` checks that each entry it names is a row of this register that names the artefact. DIV-7 is not in that field: `dss-divergence` records a result of DSS that differs from the prediction of `expected-dss`, and for `mutations/pades-incremental-update`, `mutations/pades-shadow-hide`, `mutations/pades-shadow-replace` and `mutations/pades-shadow-hide-and-replace` DSS gives the result predicted. The difference is between sigillum and DSS, which the field cannot express, even once the revision analysis of phase 2 exists. The notes of these four artefacts name DIV-7 instead, and a test of `sigillum-testpki` checks that every entry named in the notes of an artefact is a row of this register that names it.

The corpus results of DSS 6.5.1 are compared with the predictions of `testdata/manifest.toml` under the aligned validation policy of [ADR-0023](adr/0023-validation-policy-of-the-dss-oracle.md). That policy resolves, as parameters that ETSI EN 319 102-1 V1.4.1 leaves to the policy, the differences on Ed25519, revocation freshness, unknown critical extensions and `id-pkix-ocsp-nocheck`, so they are not divergences.
