### Added

- The `sign` mode of `tools/dss-oracle`, which creates CAdES, PAdES and JAdES baseline signatures at B-B, B-T and B-LT with DSS, from a test key, a fixed signing time and offline sources only. B-T timestamps come from `sigillum-testpki tsa`, run without a shell with the digest that DSS requests and a configured time, so no clock is read. At B-LT, DSS embeds the CRLs and OCSP responses supplied, selected and checked at a given time, never the time of the clock, under the aligned validation policy: a CRL or OCSP response that fails the checks is never embedded, and the run fails when no accepted revocation data remains for a certificate that needs it. DSS takes the CRLs and OCSP responses in the order given. Every JAdES signature carries `x5c` in its protected header, as Annex I of Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/248 of 2 February 2026 (http://data.europa.eu/eli/reg_impl/2026/248/oj) requires. Signatures are deterministic, and the mode writes a signature only when DSS finds it intact.
- `sigillum-testpki generate documents`, which writes the base documents of the signed corpus under `testdata/documents/`: a small PDF, a PDF with an empty signature field, a text payload and a JSON payload, written directly from ISO 32000-1:2008 and reproducible byte for byte.
- `sigillum-testpki generate signed`, which drives the `sign` mode to create the signed corpus under `testdata/signed/`: CAdES enveloping and detached, PAdES, and JAdES in the flattened and general serialisations at B-B, B-T and B-LT, and JAdES compact at B-B, for RSA PKCS#1 v1.5, RSASSA-PSS, ECDSA P-256 and Ed25519. Each signature has a manifest entry with the DSS result predicted from ETSI EN 319 102-1 V1.4.1. The manifest gains the link `timestamp-chain`, which names the certificate of the timestamping authority and the chain above it for signatures with a signature timestamp.
- A workflow that regenerates the signed corpus without network access and checks that it is unchanged byte for byte.
- `sigillum-testpki verify --openssl` checks the CAdES signatures of the signed corpus with `openssl cms -verify` at the validation time. A CMS signature recorded as `not-applicable` is refused, and PAdES and JAdES signatures stay `not-applicable`.
