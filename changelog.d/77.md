### Changed

- The scope and the threat model allow SHA-1 only as a lookup identifier where a standard specifies or permits SHA-1 for that identifier, never for integrity or signatures. The cases are the hash algorithm of an OCSP `CertID` (RFC 6960, section 4.1.1), the `byKey` responder identifier (RFC 6960, sections 4.2.1 and 4.2.2.3) and key identifiers computed with method 1 of RFC 5280, section 4.2.1.2. The cryptographic policy still rejects SHA-1 everywhere else. Threat K10 names a planned fixture with a SHA-256 `CertID`.
