# Recorded divergences from DSS

This register lists every case in which sigillum intentionally produces a different result from DSS. The process is described in [conformance.md](conformance.md).

| Id | Format | Description | Standard clause | Test vector | Upstream report | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| DIV-1 | JAdES | DSS accepts detached signatures with the `ObjectIdByURI` mechanism over more than one object. sigillum reports INDETERMINATE with "not supported", because the boundary between objects is not signed. Class 3, intentional restriction ([ADR-0019](adr/0019-divergence-classes-and-intentional-restrictions.md)), decided in [ADR-0018](adr/0018-restrict-jades-payloads-and-detached-mechanisms.md) | ETSI TS 119 182-1 V1.2.1, clause 5.2.8.3.2 | To be added with the J5 fixture | Report to ETSI TC ESI drafted, not yet sent; optional under ADR-0019 | Accepted; fixture pending (J5) |
| DIV-2 | All formats | DSS is expected to accept object identifiers with an arc of 2^28 or more, such as `2.25` followed by a UUID. DSS 6.5.1 reads them through Bouncy Castle 1.86, whose `ASN1ObjectIdentifier` holds large arcs as `BigInteger` values. It accepts arcs of any size within its limit of 4096 contents octets. The DIV-2 fixture is to confirm it. sigillum reports INDETERMINATE with "not supported", in dotted text and in DER. `const-oid` 0.9.6 mishandles such arcs, as [RustCrypto/formats#1585](https://github.com/RustCrypto/formats/issues/1585) shows; [RustCrypto/formats#1592](https://github.com/RustCrypto/formats/pull/1592), [RustCrypto/formats#1600](https://github.com/RustCrypto/formats/pull/1600) and [RustCrypto/formats#1753](https://github.com/RustCrypto/formats/pull/1753) fixed the dotted form on master. In dotted form, an arc of 2^28 or more that fits in 32 bits can parse and then panic on display or arc iteration. The arc 2^28 is encoded as 0, so `1.2.268435456` gives `1.2.0`. Above 32 bits, release builds wrap silently, so `1.2.4294967301` gives `1.2.5`. In DER, an arc above 32 bits can be truncated silently, so `2A 90 80 80 80 05` gives `1.2.5` ([RustCrypto/formats#2412](https://github.com/RustCrypto/formats/pull/2412)). Class 3, intentional restriction ([ADR-0019](adr/0019-divergence-classes-and-intentional-restrictions.md)), decided in [ADR-0024](adr/0024-bounded-decoding-of-untrusted-input.md). The limit also guards against the truncation in DER. It is lifted only when a `const-oid` release used by sigillum contains all five fixes: [RustCrypto/formats#1522](https://github.com/RustCrypto/formats/pull/1522), [RustCrypto/formats#1592](https://github.com/RustCrypto/formats/pull/1592), [RustCrypto/formats#1600](https://github.com/RustCrypto/formats/pull/1600), [RustCrypto/formats#1753](https://github.com/RustCrypto/formats/pull/1753) and [RustCrypto/formats#2412](https://github.com/RustCrypto/formats/pull/2412). This applies to the DER path and to the dotted path alike. Lifting it relaxes the four-octet rule for subidentifiers of the walk and the arc limit of the dotted syntax. The syntax and round-trip checks of ADR-0024 stay. Under the general rule of ADR-0024, rule 4, any untrusted identifier that fails those checks or parsing by `const-oid` also gives "not supported". This includes the structural limits of `const-oid` 0.9.6: 3 to 39 contents octets, and a second arc of at most 39 under root 2 as well | ITU-T X.660 (07/2011), clause 7.6, leaves arcs unbounded; ITU-T X.690 (02/2021), clause 8.19 | To be added with the first story that parses untrusted object identifiers, in DER or dotted text, in practice the first that decodes untrusted DER | None needed for DSS; the defects are public upstream | Accepted; fixture pending |
| DIV-3 | X.509, OCSP | DSS 6.5.1 accepts an OCSP response signed by a certificate that is neither the CA nor a delegated responder with `id-kp-OCSPSigning`, once it has revocation data for that signer, and reports PASSED. sigillum rejects the response. Class 1, required by the standard ([ADR-0019](adr/0019-divergence-classes-and-intentional-restrictions.md)): DSS accepts what the standard forbids. No constraint of the DSS 6.5.1 policy schema can require `id-kp-OCSPSigning` of delegated responders only, so the aligned policy cannot resolve it ([ADR-0023](adr/0023-validation-policy-of-the-dss-oracle.md)) | RFC 6960, section 3.2, item 4, and section 4.2.2.2 | `ocsp/responder-without-ocsp-signing` given with `crl/ca-ecdsa`, which covers its signer `pki/signer-p384` (`ValidateCertificateTest`) | Required; drafted, not yet sent | Recorded; report pending |
| DIV-4 | X.509, OCSP, CRL | When a supplied OCSP response covers the certificate but gives the status unknown, or its signer cannot be validated because the certificate of the responder carried in the response has an altered signature, DSS 6.5.1 does not use a supplied CRL that covers the same certificate. It reports INDETERMINATE with CERTIFICATE_CHAIN_GENERAL_FAILURE, while the CRL shows the certificate not revoked. When the response's own signature does not verify, DSS sets the response aside and uses the CRL, so that case is not part of this divergence. sigillum uses another acceptable source of revocation data when one exists, and reports PASSED in these cases. Class 2, DSS behaviour appears incorrect | ETSI EN 319 102-1 V1.4.1, clause 5.2.6.4, step 4 | `ocsp/unknown`, or `ocsp/signer-p256` with the signature of the responder certificate in its `certs` altered, each given with `crl/ca-ecdsa` (`ValidateCertificateTest`) | Required; drafted, not yet sent | Recorded; report pending |
| DIV-5 | X.509 | For an expired certificate whose revocation data were issued at or before its `notAfter` and show it not revoked, DSS 6.5.1 reports INDETERMINATE with OUT_OF_BOUNDS_NOT_REVOKED. Such data show no status after the certificate expired: no CRL carries expiredCertsOnCRL and no response archiveCutoff. NOTE 8 of clause 5.2.6.4, which is informative, describes a certificate known not to have been revoked as one whose CA keeps providing status after expiry, checked, or whose CA assures that it was not revoked at signing time, so sigillum predicts OUT_OF_BOUNDS_NO_POE. A CRL with expiredCertsOnCRL (ITU-T X.509 (10/2019), clause 9.5.2.8) gives OUT_OF_BOUNDS_NOT_REVOKED in both. Class 2, DSS behaviour appears inconsistent with NOTE 8 | ETSI EN 319 102-1 V1.4.1, clause 5.2.6.4, step 7 and NOTE 8 | `pki/signer-expired`, `ocsp/signer-expired`, `pki/ca-expired`, `ocsp/signer-expired-before-expiry`; `crl/ca-ecdsa-expired-certs` as the control (`ValidateCertificateTest`) | Required; drafted, not yet sent | Recorded; report pending |

The corpus results of DSS 6.5.1 are compared with the predictions of `testdata/manifest.toml` under the aligned validation policy of [ADR-0023](adr/0023-validation-policy-of-the-dss-oracle.md). That policy resolves, as parameters that ETSI EN 319 102-1 V1.4.1 leaves to the policy, the differences on Ed25519, revocation freshness, unknown critical extensions and `id-pkix-ocsp-nocheck`, so they are not divergences.
